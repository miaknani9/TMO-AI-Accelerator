More about how this works & customising domains
Each domain is scored strictly against the evidence pasted or dropped into its own box. Rename, remove or add domains to match your client's own maturity framework (2–16 domains recommended), and optionally add scoring criteria to define what "good" looks like for a domain. Note: the Transformation Coherence checks are tied to specific pairs of the default domain names — renaming or removing domains heavily may reduce how many of those checks still apply. The artefact note under each box is our first attempt at saying what tends to evidence that domain well — a working draft, not a checklist to complete before you start; flag gaps or mis-categorisation to the tool owner rather than treating it as final.

      The default component list and the coherence/red-flag model are adapted from Capgemini's internal Outcome-Led Programme Structuring methodology (enterprise/delivery components, and the five named failure modes behind why transformations lose coherence). The structuring check (Frame/Architect/Decompose/Structure) uses the same methodology's stage names, but the specific recommended activities, indicative durations and roadmap sequencing are an illustrative first pass written for this prototype, not validated decision logic from the methodology owners — treat these as a starting point to react to and refine, not an agreed way of working. This is a working interpretation built from a summary of the methodology, not the full, formally validated framework — the assessment criteria, coherence checks, structuring logic and wording should all be reviewed and signed off by the methodology owners and assurance leads before client use, and can be customised per account using the editor above. The 12 components themselves (6 Enterprise Components, 6 Delivery Components) and their grouping follow Capgemini's own TMO operating model ("Be transformation ready") — the structuring roadmap also tags each recommended activity with the single component it most relates to, as additional framing that does not change any stage, status, evidenceLevel or duration. The Project & Programme Management (PMO) component's governance criteria additionally reflects a leadership discussion on governance best practice — decision rights and hierarchy, an outcomes/benefits focus rather than status reporting, accurate and decision-focused data, discipline about what's discussed where, and governance's role in enabling timely challenge, escalation or stop decisions. Capgemini's own methodology is intended as a further framework option here once provided.

      Any component, structuring stage, coherence pair or red flag with no supporting evidence in what you provided is marked "Not assessed" rather than given a manufactured rating — add more detail and re-run for fuller coverage. Where evidence does support a rating, a short verbatim quote is shown underneath so the assessment can be traced back to its source. Each recommendation is also tagged with the component(s) it most relates to and, where the AI judged one recommendation as a practical precondition for another, a "Depends on" note — this sequencing is an AI judgement call to aid prioritisation discussions, not a validated delivery plan, and should be sense-checked rather than followed mechanically. Every component's 1-5 score is scaled against Capgemini's Transformation Maturity Model (Foundational/Developing/Embedded/Performant/Transformational), reproduced under "What do Foundational → Transformational mean?" above — this is the same model used elsewhere in client proposals, adapted here as a shared scoring rubric across all 12 components rather than a single organisation-wide rating.

      The optional "organisational & programme context" prompts in Step 2 (leadership stability, culture, strategy changes, documented strategy/benefits framework and so on) exist because evidence alone often can't tell a genuine planning or delivery gap from the same symptom caused by leadership turnover, repeated strategy resets, or external pressure — answering them only changes how a finding's rationale or root cause is explained, never any score, status, RAG or evidence rating, and skipping all of them changes nothing about how the rest of the tool behaves.

      The programme-stage selector uses Capgemini's official TMO lifecycle naming (Strategy/Design/Build/Run/Handover, per the P&CA TMO Proposition), the intervention tier recommendation is based on the real Assess/Diagnose/Recover model from the P&CA Transformation Assurance & Recovery proposition, and the prioritisation matrix reflects the same deliverable named there — all three are illustrative readings against the evidence, not a verbatim reproduction of either proposition's full criteria, and should be checked against those decks directly before client use. The optional framework alignment (MSP / P3O / APM / UK Government Standard for Project Delivery) only re-phrases and tags findings and recommendations in that framework's own terminology — it never changes a score, status, RAG, evidenceLevel, timeframe, impact or effort rating. The framework summaries and tag lists shown are a consultant-authored working summary for orientation, not a verbatim reproduction of the official guidance, and the tagging itself is an AI judgement call, not a certified mapping — check the current official publication before treating any of it as a compliance or certification claim.

      A simple pattern-based check flags text that looks like it may contain real identifiable information (not a compliance tool — always follow data handling policy and check with your Data Privacy Officer for real client data).
